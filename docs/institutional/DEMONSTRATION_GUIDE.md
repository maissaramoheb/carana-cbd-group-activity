# Institutional demonstration guide

Prepare a clean browser profile with synthetic demonstration data. Identify the exact release and its validation status before starting. A suggested ten-minute discussion follows; adapt to the stakeholder's training role.

1. State the problem and the independent-adaptation boundary. This is a working learning environment, not an official UN-endorsed course or proof of measured learning gains.
2. Open CBD Lesson 1. Show its objectives and a short explanation. Play a few seconds of disclosed AI narration, then open the transcript to demonstrate an alternative.
3. Open the visual gallery and compare two PESTEL-S dimensions. Ask what changes in the adviser's analytical question. Show the triangulation triangle and how observations, interviews and records differ.
4. Show one cinematic situation paired with a concept. Explain why only three were selected in Lesson 1: context and professional judgement, with interactive concepts doing most explanatory work.
5. Try a formative choice and discuss its feedback. Explain the difference from checkpoint mastery and from observed workplace competence. Do not use an answer key as a demonstration shortcut.
6. Show the 5×6 matrix prompt, then open the original practical activity. Explain the preserved standalone workbench, locally stored records and self-confirmed practical completion.
7. Show the expansion catalog or demo only with accurate labels: Lesson 1 working implementation; additional CBD lessons development/review; MMA/COP readiness until their own acceptance gates close.
8. Close on the partnership decision: curriculum and rights reviewers, bounded sponsorship scope, accessibility/user validation and sustainable ownership. The real-learner pilot is deferred; discuss the proposal without implying authorisation.

If audio or hosting fails, use the transcript and locally served build. Do not treat an SSO redirect on a protected preview as a course login requirement. Never change production aliases during a demonstration. Questions about institutional adoption, copyright or procurement go to the designated authority rather than being represented as resolved.
