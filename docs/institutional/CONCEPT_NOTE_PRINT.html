CARANA E LEARNING · INDEPENDENT DEVELOPMENT

Institutional partnership
concept note

Partnership proposition

A reusable interactive digital learning environment designed to improve access to, consistency of, and practical application within UN Police job-specific training. We seek curriculum review and a scoped development partnership to complete the CBD adaptation and prepare carefully bounded MMA and COP conversion work. Formal institutional adoption, rights permission and learner validation are separate decisions.

Need and response

Experienced police advisers need opportunities to interpret evidence, choose an approach, consider consequences and connect concepts with professional practice. Classroom lesson plans also depend on facilitation, group work and debriefing. Digitising the materials therefore requires more than reproducing slides. The proposed environment combines clear explanations, conceptual exploration, applied choices with feedback and practical workbench activities. It can support preparation, facilitated sessions and later reference. It cannot establish professional competence through screen completion alone.

Working implementation

CBD Lesson 1, Situational Analysis, is the working example: six sections, 18 instructional screens, 18 AI narration recordings with transcripts, 36 checkpoint questions, interactive analytical tools, three cinematic teaching scenes and a link to the original CARANA practical activity. Technical evidence includes automated checks and browser journeys; the original activity source was preserved. Browser-local progress works without compulsory accounts. It is device- and origin-specific and is not a verified qualification record.

Curriculum and intended users

The initial audience is UN Police practitioners, trainers and relevant police capacity-development counterparts. Additional CBD lessons are being developed from official lesson plans with objective-to-content-to-assessment traceability. Monitoring, Mentoring and Advising and Community-Oriented Policing are proposed further tracks; they are not presented as completed courses. The digital approach follows a professional learning rhythm: discover, explore, understand, decide, practise, reflect and apply.

Benefits to test and limits to retain

The intended benefits are more flexible access, consistent core explanations, opportunities for repeated practice and stronger links between analytical tools and practical tasks. These are hypotheses for validation, not measured outcomes. Group learning, cultural judgement and interpersonal performance still require appropriate human observation and feedback. Full accessibility conformance, rights clearance, human narration review and institutional suitability remain open gates. No field pilot results exist.

Implementation and governance

Development should proceed lesson by lesson. Each increment needs an authoritative source, an instructional map, a participant/facilitator boundary, usable practice and assessments, browser and accessibility checks and a recoverable release. The owner retains product and release control. A designated institutional curriculum authority should approve doctrine and learning alignment; a rights authority should approve permitted reuse and branding. Trainers and representative users should inform acceptance. The software team maintains reproducibility, media integrity and regression evidence.

Validation and sustainability

A real-learner pilot is deliberately deferred by the owner. A proposed later pilot would examine usability, comprehension, applied output, accessibility and transfer with informed consent and proportionate data collection. Its findings should guide revision before institutional adoption. Sustainability requires a named curriculum steward, a maintained source and release register, operating support, periodic browser/accessibility reviews and a funded content-update process. Hosting alone is not a sustainability model.

Funding and partnership decision

Three planning scenarios are offered: A completes the CBD adaptation; B adds initial MMA/COP conversion pilots; C establishes a broader institutional digital-learning capability and first-year operations. The budget model separates development, instructional design, curriculum expertise, media, testing, accessibility, support, maintenance and evaluation. Figures are hypothetical USD planning allowances, not quotations or approved budgets. Prospective partners can contribute expert review, training access, accessibility assessment, hosting/support or funding. The immediate decision is to agree a sponsor scope, designate curriculum and rights reviewers and authorise a costed work package. It does not authorise public endorsement or production release.
