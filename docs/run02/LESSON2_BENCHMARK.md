# Lesson2 implementation benchmark

The benchmark is an engineering and source-based instructional standard for this run, not independent curriculum approval. Its scope covers the 2023 lesson’s strategic relationships, ownership, transparent priorities, SMART objectives and performance framework; facilitated competence remains outside the digital claim.

Every important concept receives a clear explanation, named relationship, independently invented professional dilemma, feedback and a practical handoff. Use a construction or evidence task at the point where recognition is insufficient, rather than adding definitions merely to increase screen count.

Lesson2 now uses:
- A system relationship map before selecting objectives.
- A continuing-ownership explanation that invites a counterpart challenge.
- An adjustable priority comparison that exposes the effects of weights and preserves constraints. It is explicitly not the official scoring chart.
- A five-part SMART drafting scaffold with self-review, no automatic professional text grading.
- A before/after objective briefing showing why a completed activity is not an operational result.
- Classification of delivery information, practice evidence and evidence limitations.
- A worked case about non-comparable samples and a four-part indicator collection/interpretation plan.
- A handoff to the original Activity2 task: 6–10 objectives, participant chart excluding the risk category at this stage, top two phrased as SMART, followed by human discussion.

The eight screens serve distinct reasoning needs; they are not an attempt to match Lesson1’s length. Checkpoint mastery still requires correcting every item with unlimited retries. Scaffolds and illustrative classifications are formative and do not change the score. Draft content is local, capped and exported; users are prompted to use invented/non-sensitive details.

Before extension to Lessons3–6, run the complete Lesson2 journey across desktop Chromium, desktop/mobile WebKit, keyboard interaction, draft and parameter restoration, error recovery, sampled accessibility and original-activity access. Capture actual tool screenshots. Human experts must still assess source fidelity, explanatory quality and whether actual learner output is defensible.
