Stage 1 · BOPPPS Bridge-In & Setup

Context, Learning Objectives & Team Details

Estimated time: 20 minutes

Aim & Relevance

To enable participants to understand conflict analysis, response analysis, and CBD analysis as evidence-based tools to support the identification of challenges that underpin police reform under UN Sustainable Development Goal 16 (Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions).

  • Define the current "As is" state before prescribing interventions.
  • Avoid assumptions by grounding plans in documented empirical facts.
  • Establish an evidence baseline for subsequent evaluation in Phase 5.

Learning Objectives

  • Deconstruct host-State policing practice and structural incentives.
  • Analyze the political, legal, economic and security dimensions via PESTEL-S.
  • Map internal and external actors and motivations in response analysis.
  • Populate the official 5 × 6 Areas–Dimensions Matrix without duplication.
  • Funnel findings into a 2 × 2 SWOT analysis to isolate CBD points of entry.
  • Formulate verifiable baseline indicators across the 3 perspectives.

Team & Session Details

Case Briefing: Galasi Criminal Investigations Services (CIS)

You are assigned as the responsible UNPOL CBD adviser to the Galasi Criminal Investigations Services (CIS). Carana National Police (CNP) has 7,200 officers (only 500 female, ratio ~1:2000). Galasi has 3,000,000 residents including IDPs. The Galasi Police Department (PD) has 2,500 officers (~150 female), and the Galasi CIS has 250 officers, of which only 12 are female (4.8%). Officers in Galasi CIS are exclusively of Falin ethnicity.

Key challenges in Galasi include disproportionate victimization of women, children, and minorities; LGBTTQI victims afraid to report; prevalent SGBV, riots, and small arms violence; no specialized investigative units (except violent extremism); outdated colonial arrest policy; conflict with public prosecutors; lack of forensic capabilities; and suspended Code of Conduct.

Ensure syndicate consensus before proceeding.
