Definition & Mandated Authority

"The term transition describes the process of closing a CBD activity and handing it over to the national counterpart or another CBD actor (e.g. UN Country Team, UNDP, UNODC, or bilateral partners). It refers solely to isolated CBD activities on the tactical/operational level, not mission drawdown." (Lesson 6 p. 4).

  • Decision Authority (Slide 9): The Head of Police Component (HOPC) decides to initiate transition with the advice of the responsible UNPOL CBD adviser.
  • Dual Nature: Handover is not merely the final phase of a project, but the beginning of an ongoing, self-sustaining national process.
  • Sensitivity & Respect: Sensitivity on the part of the CBD adviser is crucial when coordinating with counterparts and succeeding agencies.

The Four Key Principles of Transition (Slide 8)

  • 1. Early Planning: Begins early in the project life cycle, remains flexible, and incorporates scenario-based contingency planning.
  • 2. UN Integration: Planned and coordinated with UN actors (UNCT, UNDP, UN Women, UNODC) who have an enduring interest in long-term police reform.
  • 3. Local Ownership: National counterparts lead the process; responsible institutional owners are identified and empowered to manage budgets and doctrine.
  • 4. Communication: Transparent protocols that carefully manage expectations among host-State leaders, mission staff, and civil society.
Curriculum Context (Lesson 6 Activity 6.1): Transition concerns the handover of an individual CBD activity or project (tactical/operational level), rather than full UN mission drawdown. Syndicates work from the instructional assumption that the capacity-building objectives established in Lessons 1–3 and stabilized in Lesson 5 are now advancing toward sustainable national ownership.
