The Deming Cycle & Evaluation Purpose

Evaluation is the natural counterpart to planning within the CBD cycle: "Evaluation generates empirical knowledge about progress toward achieving sustainable development: did the engagement work; what did not work and why?" (Lesson 5 p. 4).

  • Plan (Modules 1–3): Situational analysis, SMART objectives, logframe causal logic.
  • Do (Module 4): MMA execution, mentoring, and dynamic field problem-solving.
  • Check (Module 5 Stage 1–3): Empirical measurement, variance analysis against baseline/targets.
  • Act (Module 5 Stage 4–5): Adjusting activities, reallocating budgets, and adapting agreements.

Evaluation Actors & Principles

  • Evaluation Actors (Slide 7): OIOS, DPET, Mission Evaluation Unit (under Mission CoS/Support), Police Division (PD inspections), SPC, and Third-Party Evaluators.
  • Professional: Grounded in agreed United Nations principles and methods (e.g. Strategic Guidance Framework / SGF).
  • Honest & Self-Reflective: Frank acknowledgment of systemic bottlenecks and institutional shortcomings.
  • Impartial & Transparent: Engaging independent perspectives to prevent confirmation bias.
  • Human Rights & Gender Sensitive: Disaggregating data to protect vulnerable beneficiaries.

📰 "Le Galasien" Investigative Report (Passages a–g) Public Press Expose

[a. Leadership Turnover & Questionable Succession] The Head of Galasi CIS is to be transferred to another region soon. His deputy, a member of the Tatsi minority installed for UN diversity requirements, has little-to-no criminal investigation experience (served in riot control). Meanwhile, the external candidate for succession faces allegations of past human rights violations.

[b. Slow Progress] Development of UNAC initiatives to empower Galasi PD is slow; no visible results so far and none expected soon.

[c. UNAC Spokesperson Interview] UNAC spokesperson admitted little measurable progress, citing IT system problems that caused the total loss of performance measurement data, low absorption capacity in Galasi PD, and vetting and statutory law delays.

[d. Jurisdiction & Competence Struggles] The Ministry of Justice claims exclusive competence over drafting and implementing CNP policy, clashing with police leadership.

[e. Ombudsman Inactivity] Independent misconduct complaint ombudsman installed as first success, but has not been used by citizens for unknown reasons.

[f. Public Skepticism] Vulnerable groups, women, and ethnic minorities interviewed remain deeply skeptical about any genuine change in CNP behavior.

[g. Economic Imperative] Insecurity threatens commerce and investment; citizens expect tangible results from the UN presence.

📧 Official Email Communication from Section Chief Yaa (Lesson 5 p. 15) Budget Cliff Alert
"Referring to your questions about the media coverage, I just attended a meeting for unit leaders responsible for CBD activities in the UNAC police component. There is serious concern about our performance.

I have been requested to report on all CBD activities in my responsibility, including yours. Your most recent report showed that you are lagging 6 weeks behind and have not started to implement planned activities. Your plans were approved 4 months ago.

Be advised that your initial timeline was close to the end of this budgetary period. Funds cannot be carried over to the next period. That might require you to repeat the full review and approval process."

Curriculum Decision Rule (Lesson 5 Slide 10): Implementors must formally categorize their reaction to each evaluation recommendation:
  • Fully Accept: Agree entirely with the recommendation and commit immediately to concrete implementation actions.
  • Partially Accept: Agree with specific elements while rejecting others, detailing precise evidence-based reasons why parts are unfeasible.
  • Reject: Reject the recommendation, providing clear justification and alternative remedial measures.
